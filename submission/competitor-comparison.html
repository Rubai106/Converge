Workflow 1 · Business · Primary customer

HAGGLE — restaurant supplier negotiation

Primary user: an independent restaurant owner/operator facing a supplier price rise. Hypothesis about current workflow — not validated user research: owners may check invoices in a spreadsheet, estimate the dish impact, and negotiate by phone or messaging app, with a limited record of what was agreed or why. HAGGLE prices the shock dish-by-dish, generates mandate-bound strategies, and makes the owner — not the agent — approve the deal in the team room.

Measurable benefit claimed: the exact cost/serving, food-cost %, margin, and monthly/annual impact of a price change, and the maximum sustainable supplier price at a target food-cost % — calculated deterministically, not estimated by an LLM.

Read this section sceptically

  • Invoice → dish costing is well covered by the market. HAGGLE’s maths is not novel as arithmetic.
  • Its difference is packaging that maths as a negotiation calculator with a mandate and a human gate in the shared room — the gate itself is enforced by CONVERGE’s backend, not by the chat tool.
  • Demo evidence, stated plainly: DairyCo and its counter-offer are fictional demo data. The owner’s approval was real. The original Mattermost timeline was lost when the VM was replaced; CONVERGE’s approval and audit record survived. The rebuilt room contains an honest historical note, not the original conversation.

Workflow 2 · Personal / family · Proof the engine generalises

COURT — family recipe adjudication

Audience: a family or small group arguing over whose version of a recipe is “the” recipe. Today they cook from memory, argue from preference, and the loudest version wins. COURT makes the versions explicit, hides them behind codes, collects independent ratings, and only then reveals the tally — preserving the losing version instead of deleting it.

COURT is not a statistical study. Professional tools below use ≥24 panellists for discrimination tests and ≥60 for hedonic tests. COURT is a deliberation tool for a small group.

Why COURT matters to the pitch

  • BakeProfit Test Kitchen already combines recipe trials and blind tasting — COURT does not claim that combination as novel.
  • Its narrower claim is the deliberation layer: multi-party deliberation, server-side withholding of identities and tally, disagreement analysis, and a canonical record preserving dissent.
  • It also shows the engine is not “a restaurant tool with extras” — the conflict is taste, not money, and the same gates still apply.

  • Single-user version history & side-by-side diff (Chef Notebook)
  • Side-by-side recipe trials + blind tasting cards, sensory ratings, taster comments and adopting a winner (BakeProfit Test Kitchen)
  • Blind coded tasting as a party game (Palate Party) and for drinks (Blind Taster)
  • Enterprise sensory panels with statistics (Compusense etc.)
  • Generic group voting & proposals (Loomio, Miro)

② Actually built in CONVERGE

  • Conflict table comparing 3 versions of one recipe
  • Blind test-cook with coded samples S1 / S2 / S3

Workflow 3 · Creator / editorial · Third application

CORRECTION — podcast claim challenges & errata

Audience: a podcast host and their guest/producer. Today, if a guest disputes something said on air, the fix is manual and inconsistent: a note in the show notes, a replaced audio file (downloaded copies stay wrong), or a mention next episode. There is no formal “challenge this claim” step. CORRECTION formalises it — without pretending to be an AI fact-checker.

Honesty boundary: extraction in CONVERGE is rule-based and auditable. It separates factual assertions from opinions and links each claim to evidence the participants supply. It does not generate a truth verdict.

Position CORRECTION carefully

  • Do not pitch it as competing with Full Fact or Logically — they serve professional fact-checkers at scale.
  • Pitch it as the missing last mile: what a small show does after a claim is challenged.
  • The research found no product for guest challenge → host approval → errata. The practice exists only as editorial policy pages.

① Already in the market

  • Transcription & text-based editing (Descript, Riverside, Otter, Trint)
  • AI claim detection & triage for fact-checkers (Full Fact AI, Logically Accelerate, ClaimBuster)
  • Search of already-published fact checks (Google Fact Check Explorer)
  • Manual errata practice: show-notes note, file replacement, next-episode correction

② Actually built in CONVERGE

  • Rule-based extraction of factual claims from a transcript (claims vs. opinions)

  • Guest challenges a claim in the shared chat room; verification agent records the challenge

What a competitor could copy — honestly

  • Easy to copy: the six-strategy list, the 6-dimension rating form, the errata template. None is a moat on its own.
  • Harder to copy: deterministic maths + mandate + counter-offer repricing inside a persistent team room, with the human-only rule enforced by CONVERGE’s backend (agent approval attempts are rejected) and every step audited. Switch / Mattermost provides the shared room and message delivery — it does not itself enforce the authorisation rule.
  • Why return? Every new price shock, recipe dispute, or challenged claim re-enters the same room and record. The value compounds as the audit history — what was agreed, by whom, on what evidence — grows.
  • Why not just chat + spreadsheet? Standalone chat on its own does not price the decision, structure a mandate, or produce an approval record — though teams can add bots, approvals and other tools around it. A standalone spreadsheet can calculate, and can be configured with sharing, comments or approval add-ons, but on its own it is not a shared negotiation with a counter-party, a backend-enforced approval gate, and preserved dissent in one record. CONVERGE’s claim is that conjunction in one workflow, not that chat or spreadsheets are useless.
