# CONVERGE — Competitor Validation

**Dates:** 2026-11-05 (web search + vendor-page opens) and 2026-11-06 (deep research pass, index-sourced, report at `~/workspace/research_notes/converge-court-competitors-20261106-0453/report.md`). Prices are as published in the cited sources and may have changed; third-party figures are flagged as such. No "CONVERGE is first" claim is made — the honest claim is narrower, per workflow, and is phrased as **"our research did not identify a single product combining the complete workflow"**, not "no such product exists anywhere".

## Verdict

Almost every named competitor exists — except **Sofra** and **Happi Happi**, which could not be found and must not be cited, and **Cupi**, whose product is unverified (see §4). **HAGGLE is the most crowded**: invoice→dish-margin is a solved $59/mo commodity (PlateSense/Marji) and autonomous negotiation is shipped at enterprise scale (Pactum) and for restaurants (SupplyScout). **COURT is the least crowded**: the closest pieces are a single-user recipe app with version comparison (Chef Notebook) and a party game with blind tasting (Palate Party) — no one adjudicates conflicting versions of one dish. **CORRECTION is split**: transcript editing/show notes are solved (Riverside/Descript), claim extraction exists for professional fact-checkers (Full Fact AI, Logically Accelerate), but no product lets the *guest* correct timestamped claims in chat with a host-approved errata. CONVERGE's defensible claim is the **assembly**: one shared decision engine, in-chat multi-party work, deterministic maths/evidence, and a human approval gate, in Mattermost via Switch, for users who will never install a portal.

**CORRECTION differentiation:** do not claim "first fact-checking for podcasts" — DOAC does it internally, Full Fact AI and Logically do the extraction step for professionals, and Kodavara/show.fm do the pre-record half. **Podcast errata as a practice (verified, no product):** corrections are handled manually and inconsistently — a dated correction note at the top of the show notes, replacing the audio file in the host (already-downloaded copies stay wrong), or announcing the correction at the top of the next episode. No product was found that automates any of this, or that routes a guest's challenge through a chat room to a host approval. ## 5. What not to claim

1. Not "first" at recipe costing, supplier negotiation, blind tasting, or podcast fact-checking — each component is shipped somewhere. Phrase the novelty as the *combination*: "our research did not identify a single product combining the complete workflow" — never "no such product exists anywhere".
2. Not statistically valid sensory science for COURT — a family panel is a deliberation tool, not a study; say so.
3. Not a real supplier agreement unless a real supplier accepted it — label the demo supplier as simulated.
4. Do not cite **Sofra**, **Happi Happi**, or **Cupi** — not found / unverified. Do not cite **ChefHero** (rebranded as Notch) or **Podcastle** (rebranded as Async) under their old names. Do not cite **SmartSensory** as one product, or **Factmata** as a current product.
5. Do not cite market-size statistics — none is sourced in either pass (an earlier draft's sensory-market figure was removed for this reason).
6. The shared-engine claim is the defensible one: one decision engine (case → evidence → conflict → proposal → approval → audited outcome) reused across three workflows, with Switch rooms as the collaboration surface and deterministic tools for money/maths. No named competitor offers that *combination* in Mattermost via Switch for these users — phrase it as an assembly claim, not a first-ever claim.
